What is a holdover tenant?

A holdover tenant is one who stays in the property after the lease term has ended without signing a new one. They are not a trespasser, and what happens next depends almost entirely on one thing: whether the landlord accepts rent.

The definition

A holdover tenant, sometimes called a tenant at sufferance, is a tenant who remains in possession after their lease term expires without the landlord's express agreement to a new term. The status is temporary: it resolves within a rent cycle or two into either a new tenancy or an eviction, and which one it becomes is usually decided by the landlord's own conduct.

Not the same as a squatter

A holdover entered lawfully under a valid lease. That is why the landlord cannot simply remove them and must go through the same notice and court process as with any other tenant.

What happens next: the fork

In most states, if the landlord accepts rent after the term has ended, a new periodic tenancy is created — usually month-to-month, on the same terms as the expired lease. If the landlord refuses rent and serves notice, the holdover is a basis to recover possession.

  1. The term ends and the tenant staysHoldover status begins. Nothing has been decided yet.
  2. The landlord choosesAccept rent, or refuse it and serve notice. This is the decision point, and it is often made by accident — a standing bank transfer arrives and nobody stops it.
  3. If rent is acceptedMost states treat a new month-to-month tenancy as created on the old terms, including the old rent. Ending it then requires proper notice, and raising the rent requires a separate increase notice.
  4. If rent is refused and notice servedThe landlord serves a notice to vacate and, if the tenant does not leave, files a holdover eviction action. Only a court can order removal.
Accepting rent by inertia is still accepting rent

A landlord intending to recover possession should stop the payment or return it promptly and in writing. Banking a payment while telling the tenant to leave is contradictory, and in many states it is the fact a court relies on to find that a new tenancy was created.

Holdover rent clauses

Many American leases contain a holdover clause setting an increased rent for any period after the term ends — commonly 125 to 200 per cent of the previous rent, charged monthly or sometimes daily. It exists to price the disruption of a tenant who will not leave when the landlord has committed the property to somebody else.

Enforceability varies. Courts generally uphold a holdover rent that is a genuine estimate of the landlord's loss, and are much less willing to enforce one that operates as a penalty. Several states cap holdover charges or prohibit them in residential tenancies altogether.

If your lease has one, read it before the term ends rather than after. If you are drafting one, keep the multiple within a range that reflects an actual cost — a plausible clause that is enforced is worth considerably more than an aggressive one that is not.

How to avoid holdover entirely

  • Diary the term end at ninety days. Every decision that avoids a holdover is made in that window and none of them can be made in the last fortnight.
  • Send the renewal offer early. Sixty to ninety days out, stating the new term and rent. See lease renewal agreement.
  • If you are not renewing, say so in writing, with whatever notice the lease and your state require.
  • Decide in advance whether month-to-month is acceptable. For many landlords it is, and then holdover is not a problem but a default worth leaving in place.
  • Write the holdover clause into the lease so both sides know the consequence before it arises, rather than discovering it in a dispute.

Frequently asked questions

Is a holdover tenant trespassing?

No. They entered lawfully under a valid lease, which is why they cannot simply be removed. A landlord who changes the locks on a holdover tenant is committing an unlawful eviction in essentially every state, with the same statutory penalties as any other self-help eviction.

What happens if my landlord keeps taking rent after my lease ends?

In most states you are now a month-to-month tenant on the terms of the expired lease, including the same rent, until either side gives proper notice or a new agreement is signed. That is usually a comfortable position, but it is also one either side can end on relatively short notice — so do not treat it as equivalent to another year.

Can a landlord charge double rent for a holdover period?

Only if the lease provides for it and the state permits it. Courts are more willing to enforce a holdover rent that reflects a real loss than one that reads as a penalty, and several states cap or prohibit such charges in residential tenancies. Check the lease clause and your state before relying on the figure.

How do I remove a holdover tenant?

Stop accepting rent, serve the notice your state requires for a holdover, and if they still do not leave, file a holdover eviction action and obtain a judgment and a writ. The process mirrors any other eviction: only a sheriff or marshal executes it, and no shortcut is lawful. See notice to vacate.

Does the security deposit carry over?

Yes. If the tenancy has converted to month-to-month, the deposit continues to be held under the original lease terms, and the statutory return deadline runs from the eventual end of the tenancy — not from the date the fixed term expired.
