Sublease agreement

A sublease lets a tenant hand their home to someone else for part of the term while staying on the hook for the original lease. The landlord's tenant does not change. A second tenancy is simply built underneath the first.

What is a sublease agreement?

A sublease is an agreement between an existing tenant and a new occupant, in which the tenant rents out all or part of the property for a period shorter than the remaining lease term. The original tenant becomes the sublandlord. Their own lease with the property owner continues untouched, and they remain fully liable under it.

That last point is the whole shape of the document. If the subtenant stops paying, the landlord does not pursue the subtenant, because the landlord has no contract with them. The landlord pursues the original tenant, who then has to pursue the subtenant. The tenant has not transferred their obligation; they have added a second one.

Sublease or assignment

A sublease transfers part of the term and keeps the original tenant in the chain. An assignment transfers the entire remaining term and, if the landlord releases the original tenant in writing, takes them out of it. If somebody is leaving permanently and wants to be done, an assignment with a written release is what they actually want, not a sublease.

Do you need the landlord's permission to sublet?

Almost always, yes. Most residential leases either prohibit subletting outright or require the landlord's prior written consent, and subletting in breach of that clause is a lease violation that can be grounds for eviction of the original tenant. Check the lease before anything else.

Some states soften a blanket prohibition: a number of jurisdictions require that consent not be unreasonably withheld, and a few cities give tenants an affirmative right to sublet under stated conditions. Even there, the process usually requires the tenant to give written notice, name the proposed subtenant, and wait a defined period for the landlord to respond.

Permission goes in writing, not on a phone call

A consent given verbally is the first thing that gets disputed when something goes wrong. Ask for consent in writing, naming the subtenant and the dates, and keep the reply. It costs one email and settles the only question that would otherwise be contested.

What has to be in a sublease

A sublease needs everything a lease needs, plus three things a lease does not: an explicit statement that the master lease governs, a copy of the master lease attached, and a clear allocation of the deposit and the damage risk between tenant and subtenant.

  • The parties and the roles. The original tenant named as sublandlord, the subtenant named as subtenant, and the property owner named for reference even though they are not a party.
  • The sublet period. Start and end dates that fall entirely inside the remaining term of the master lease. A sublease cannot outlast the lease it sits under.
  • What is being sublet. The whole unit, or one bedroom plus shared use of the kitchen, bathroom and living areas. Be specific; this is the clause that prevents most shared-housing arguments.
  • Rent. How much, to whom, and by when — and whether the subtenant pays the sublandlord or pays the landlord directly on the sublandlord's behalf.
  • Deposit. Who holds the subtenant's deposit, what it covers, and when it comes back. The original tenant's own deposit with the landlord is a separate sum and stays where it is.
  • Incorporation of the master lease. The subtenant agrees to be bound by the master lease's rules, with a copy attached. Without this, a subtenant who breaks a house rule has broken nothing they agreed to.
  • Condition report. Signed photographs at the start and the end of the sublet. The original tenant is the one who will be billed for damage, so this record protects them, not the landlord.

What the original tenant is still responsible for

Everything. For the duration of the sublet, the original tenant owes the landlord the full rent, remains responsible for damage beyond normal wear and tear, and remains answerable for lease violations committed by the subtenant, including noise, unauthorised pets and extra occupants.

This is why the screening step matters more in a sublease than almost anywhere else. A landlord screening a tenant risks a vacancy; a tenant screening a subtenant risks their own credit and their own deposit. Run the same rental application process a landlord would, and do not skip it because the person is a friend of a friend.

It is also why the sublease should give the sublandlord a clear route to end it: a defined notice period, and an explicit statement that the sublease terminates automatically if the master lease terminates for any reason.

Common mistakes

A sublease goes wrong in one direction almost every time: the original tenant behaves as though they have left the deal, when they have not.

  • Subletting without written consentMost leases require it and many prohibit subletting outright. Consent given verbally by an agent is the version nobody can produce later.
  • Letting the sublease outlast the head leaseA sublease cannot run past the tenancy it sits inside. A date beyond it is a promise the original tenant has no power to keep.
  • Charging more than the head rent where that is restrictedSome cities cap or prohibit subletting at a profit, and a rent-stabilised unit almost always does.
  • Confusing a sublease with an assignmentAn assignment transfers the tenancy and can release you. A sublease does not: you stay liable to the landlord for rent and for damage.

Red flags

What a prospective subtenant should notice before handing anybody money.

  • No copy of the head lease offeredThe head lease sets every limit on what the sublease can promise you. Someone unwilling to show it may be subletting in breach of it.
  • The landlord has never heard of youIf the landlord cannot confirm the arrangement, your occupation may end at the moment they discover it.
  • The deposit is paid to someone who is not the landlordMoney held by the original tenant is money you recover from the original tenant, wherever they are by then. See security deposit.
  • The dates do not fit inside the head leaseA sublease ending after the head lease cannot deliver the last part of what it sells.

Frequently asked questions

Can my landlord evict me for subletting without permission?

If the lease prohibits subletting or requires consent, subletting without it is a breach, and in most states a breach of a material lease term is grounds to begin eviction proceedings. Whether a landlord would is a different question from whether they could. The risk is not worth the fortnight it takes to ask.

Who returns the subtenant's deposit?

The sublandlord — the original tenant — because that is who took it. Whether they get their own deposit back from the landlord is a separate matter settled at the end of the master lease, and the two should not be tied together in the sublease. A subtenant who leaves the room in good condition should not have to wait for the master lease to end to be repaid.

Does a sublease need to be in writing?

It should be, always. A sublease is the one arrangement in residential renting where the person carrying the risk is not the person in the property, and an unwritten sublease leaves the original tenant with no record of what was agreed and no basis for withholding a deposit. It is also the version the landlord will ask to see.

Can I charge the subtenant more than I pay in rent?

Sometimes. Several rent-stabilised jurisdictions expressly prohibit it and impose penalties, and some leases forbid it by their terms. Where it is allowed, remember that the profit comes with the full liability for the property. Check local rules before you set the number.
