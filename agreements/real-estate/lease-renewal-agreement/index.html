Lease renewal agreement

A lease renewal agreement extends an existing tenancy for a new term. It states the new dates and the new rent and confirms that everything else in the original lease stays in force — which is why it runs to one page rather than fifteen.

What a lease renewal agreement does

A renewal agreement is a short amendment that extends the existing lease. It identifies the original lease by its date and address, states the new term's start and end dates, states the rent for the new term, lists any other change, and confirms that all remaining terms continue unchanged. Both parties sign it and it is attached to the original.

Writing a whole new lease instead is not wrong, but it is more work and it creates a risk: a new document can silently drop a clause the old one had, and if the deposit was taken under the old lease the new document has to account for it. An amendment avoids both problems.

What happens if nobody signs anything

In most states, if the term expires and the tenant stays and the landlord keeps accepting rent, the tenancy converts automatically to a month-to-month tenancy on the same terms as the expired lease. Nothing is void, nobody is trespassing, and the rent stays at the old amount until a proper increase notice is given.

For some landlords that is the preferred outcome, and it is a legitimate choice. It becomes a problem in three situations: when you wanted the certainty of another fixed year, when you wanted a higher rent and have now frozen the old one, and when the tenant assumed they had another year and you serve a thirty-day notice.

The default is not the same everywhere

A minority of states, and some leases, treat a tenant remaining after the term as a holdover on different terms, sometimes at an increased rent set by the lease itself. Read the holdover clause in your own lease before assuming it rolls to month-to-month. See holdover tenant.

Raising the rent at renewal

Renewal is the normal moment to change the rent, because the fixed term that froze it is ending. Most states require written notice of an increase a set period in advance — commonly thirty days, longer in several states and for larger increases — and where rent stabilisation applies, the size of the increase is capped regardless of notice.

  1. Check the date you have to act byWork back from the lease end date by your state's notice period, and by any notice period the lease itself imposes on either side. The lease's own requirement can be longer than the statute's.
  2. Check whether a cap appliesLocal rent stabilisation, and in some states a statewide cap, limits the increase. This is city-level law in most of the places it exists.
  3. Send the offer in writingNew term, new rent, the date by which you need an answer, and a note that the terms are otherwise unchanged.
  4. Sign the amendment, not a new leaseOne page, attached to the original, signed by both. The deposit stays where it is and does not need to be returned and retaken.
Timing can turn an increase into retaliation

In most states an increase that closely follows a tenant's complaint about conditions, or a report to a code inspector, is presumed retaliatory and unlawful. If a repair dispute is live, resolve it before the renewal offer goes out, and keep the two in separate correspondence.

What renewal does not reset

It does not reset the deposit. The money you are holding was taken under the original lease and continues to be held under it; there is no need to return and retake it, though some states require a top-up notice if the rent rises and your deposit is capped as a multiple of rent.

It does not cure an outstanding breach. Renewing a lease while a violation is unresolved can be read as accepting it. Deal with the breach first, or state expressly in the renewal that it is not waived.

And it does not update the disclosures. If your state has added a required disclosure since the original lease was signed, or your circumstances have changed — new knowledge of lead paint, a new managing agent, a pending sale — the renewal is the moment to attach the current versions.

Common mistakes

Renewal errors are errors of silence: both sides assume the tenancy simply continues on the old terms, and it does not.

  • Letting the term end with nothing signedWhat follows is decided by state law rather than by either party, usually by conversion to a periodic tenancy. See holdover tenant.
  • Putting a rent increase inside the renewal and nothing elseWhere the state requires separate notice of an increase, burying it in the renewal document does not satisfy that requirement.
  • Not re-checking the deposit against the new rentWhere the cap is a multiple of rent, an increase can put an existing deposit over the line. See security deposit.
  • Treating renewal as a fresh startRenewal continues a tenancy. Existing arrears, existing damage and the original condition record all carry across unless the document says otherwise.

Red flags

What to read closely in a renewal offered to you, particularly one that arrives late.

  • More has changed than the dates and the rentA renewal is the easiest moment to insert a term nobody would have signed at the start. Compare it against the original clause by clause.
  • Renewal conditional on waiving somethingA renewal offered only if you give up a repair claim or a deposit dispute is a settlement, and it should be priced as one.
  • Pressure to sign on the day the term endsThe deadline is being used as leverage. The tenancy does not usually evaporate at midnight; find out what your state says it converts to first.
  • A substantial increase with no notice periodWhere the state sets a minimum notice for increases, it applies at renewal as well.

Frequently asked questions

How much notice should I give a tenant about renewal?

Give more than the law requires. Many states require thirty to sixty days for a rent increase or a non-renewal, but sixty to ninety days before the end of the term is the practical window: it gives the tenant time to decide, and it gives you time to advertise if they decline. A renewal offer that arrives three weeks before the end of the term reads as an ultimatum whether it was meant as one or not.

Can a tenant refuse to renew and just leave at the end of the term?

Yes. A fixed-term lease ends on its end date; neither side is obliged to renew unless the lease contains a renewal option. The tenant should still confirm in writing that they are leaving and on what date, and give whatever notice the lease requires — many leases require thirty or sixty days' notice of non-renewal even though the term is ending anyway.

Can I change other terms at renewal, not just the rent?

Yes, by stating each change in the renewal agreement. Renewal is a new agreement between both parties, so anything they both accept can change — pet rules, parking, who pays which utility. What you cannot do is change a term unilaterally and treat continued occupancy as acceptance.

Does the original lease need to be re-signed?

No. The renewal amendment plus the original lease together form the agreement, provided the amendment identifies the original clearly by date and address and says the remaining terms continue. Keep both documents together; a renewal that cannot be matched to its lease is worth very little.
