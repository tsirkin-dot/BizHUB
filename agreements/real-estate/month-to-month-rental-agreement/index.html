Month-to-month rental agreement

A month-to-month rental agreement creates a tenancy that renews automatically every month until one side gives notice to end it. Same property, same rights, same protections as a year lease — different renewal cycle, and that one difference changes who carries the risk.

What is a month-to-month rental agreement?

A month-to-month rental agreement is a periodic tenancy: it runs for one month, then renews itself for another month, and keeps doing that indefinitely until either the landlord or the tenant serves notice to end it. There is no end date in the document, because the end date is whatever either side later chooses to create.

Everything else looks like a lease. Rent, deposit, utilities, pets, entry and repairs are all governed the same way, and the tenant has the same habitability rights and the same protection from lockouts. What changes is that the terms are only fixed for one cycle at a time.

You can arrive at one without signing one

In most states, if a fixed-term lease expires and the tenant stays and the landlord keeps accepting rent, the tenancy converts to month-to-month by operation of law on the old terms. That is the most common way American landlords end up in a periodic tenancy: not by choosing it, but by not doing anything. See holdover tenant.

How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy?

Thirty days is the common baseline in most states, given before the start of a rental period, but this is one of the most state-specific numbers in residential tenancy and several states require sixty days or more from the landlord for a tenant of long standing. The tenant's notice and the landlord's notice are frequently not the same length.

Three variations matter enough to check for your state before you rely on thirty days:

  • Asymmetric periods. A number of states require a longer notice from the landlord than from the tenant, often stepping up once the tenant has been there a year.
  • Notice tied to the rent cycle. In many states notice has to expire at the end of a rental period, so notice given on the tenth does not end the tenancy forty days later; it ends it at the end of the following month.
  • Just-cause jurisdictions. A growing number of cities and several states require the landlord to state a permitted reason to end a tenancy at all, in which case the notice period is only half the question.
Notice is a formal act

It has to be in writing, it has to be delivered the way the statute or the agreement specifies, and it has to state the date the tenancy ends. Keep proof of delivery. A defective notice restarts the clock, which in a month-to-month tenancy costs a full month. Use a notice to vacate rather than a text message.

Raising rent on a month-to-month tenant

Because the terms are only fixed for one cycle, a landlord can change the rent for the next cycle by giving proper written notice — commonly the same period as a termination notice, often thirty days, and longer in several states for increases above a threshold. The tenant's options are to accept the new rent or to end the tenancy.

Two limits sit on top of this. Local rent stabilisation, where it exists, caps the size of the increase regardless of notice. And retaliation statutes in most states make an increase unlawful if it follows closely after a tenant complained about conditions or contacted a code inspector, which is why the timing of an increase matters as much as its size.

A rent increase notice should state the new amount, the date it takes effect, and nothing else. Mixing it with a complaint about the tenant is how an ordinary increase turns into an argument about retaliation.

The trade is symmetrical and worth saying plainly: a landlord on month-to-month gains the ability to end or re-price the tenancy quickly, and gives up the certainty of a year of income. A tenant gains the ability to leave, and gives up the certainty that their rent will not move.

What a month-to-month agreement does not change

It does not shorten the eviction process. A month-to-month tenant who does not leave after a valid notice still has to be removed through the court process in every state; the notice simply establishes that the tenancy has ended.

It does not reduce the landlord's repair obligations, and it does not let either side skip the deposit rules. Deposit caps, holding requirements and return deadlines apply identically.

And it does not let a landlord end a tenancy for a prohibited reason. Fair-housing law, retaliation statutes and local just-cause ordinances all apply to a thirty-day notice exactly as they apply to a refusal to renew a lease.

Common mistakes

A periodic tenancy is easy to create and easy to mishandle, because nearly everything about it turns on notice given properly and counted correctly.

  • Counting the notice period from the wrong dayMany states run the period to the end of a rental period rather than from the day the letter was sent. Counting from the wrong day produces a notice that is simply invalid.
  • Raising the rent in a text or a conversationAn increase on a periodic tenancy generally needs its own written notice, with its own notice period, separate from the notice that would end the tenancy.
  • Assuming month-to-month means somebody can be asked to leave quicklyThe tenancy is flexible; the removal process is not. Ending the agreement and recovering possession remain two different things. See notice to vacate.
  • Letting a fixed term lapse without deciding anythingIn most states the tenancy converts rather than ends, usually to month-to-month. See holdover tenant.

Red flags

Signals that the flexibility of a periodic tenancy is being used against one side rather than shared by both.

  • A notice period that is not reciprocalThirty days from the tenant and seven from the landlord is a term many states will not enforce, and a statement of intent either way.
  • Increases arriving faster than the notice period allowsA second increase before the first has taken effect suggests the notice rules are not being followed at all.
  • “This agreement may be terminated immediately”No ordinary residential tenancy in the United States ends on no notice outside a court process.
  • A move to month-to-month proposed just before a saleFlexibility that appears when the property is about to be marketed is flexibility for the seller.

Frequently asked questions

Is a month-to-month agreement legally binding?

Yes. It is a binding contract for each monthly period, renewed automatically. The tenant owes the rent for any period that has begun, and the landlord owes the same habitability and quiet-enjoyment obligations as under any lease. The only thing that is not binding is what happens after the current period, and that is by design.

My fixed-term lease just ended and nobody signed anything. What are we on?

In most states, if the tenant stays and you accept rent, the tenancy has converted to month-to-month on the same terms as the expired lease. That is usually fine, but it means your rent is frozen at the old amount until you give a proper increase notice, and either side can end it on short notice. If you want another fixed term, use a lease renewal agreement instead of letting it roll.

Can I switch a tenant from a lease to month-to-month?

At the end of the term, yes, by simply not renewing and letting it convert, or by signing a new month-to-month agreement that states the change. Mid-term you cannot do it unilaterally: the fixed term binds you too. Both sides can agree to end the lease early and start a month-to-month arrangement, and that agreement should be in writing.

Does a month-to-month tenant get their security deposit back the same way?

Yes, on exactly the same terms. The deposit rules do not vary with the type of tenancy. The clock on returning it starts when the tenancy ends and possession is returned, and the same itemisation requirements apply. See getting your security deposit back.

Can I give notice in the middle of a month?

You can serve it mid-month, but in many states it will not take effect until the end of the following rental period. If your state ties notice to the rent cycle, notice served on 10 March ends the tenancy on 30 April, not 9 April. Check your state's rule before you plan a move-out date around it.
